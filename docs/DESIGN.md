# Zenkoverse: Pact — design

## Goal

A proof of concept that the Zenkoverse power system plays well as a game (issue #48). The player trains in one walled dojo compound at dusk. They fight, experiment, and learn the power system by doing: Techniques, Pacts and the Workshop, Resolve, the full hand sign, Maximum Output, drills, the tutorial and the rooftop trial. Each Technique has visible hands and body motion and physically changes the world. Polish goes to the fights and to the readability of the power system, not to world size: a very well-built small area beats a half-finished city. Finish each system before adding another, and leave nothing that reads as placeholder. Anything that looks generic or "AI-made" counts as a bug.

The private wiki lives at `../Zenkoverse` (outside this repo). Read it for content. Never copy its files into the repo.

## Art direction

**A living manga panel: a coloured manga page in motion.** The whole frame, characters, world and the first-person arms alike, is drawn the same way, in two places: the surfaces' paint (`src/render/tone.ts`) and the page over them (`src/render/manga.ts`):

- Flat colour and screentone. Every surface is a `MeshToonMaterial` whose lighting is rewritten so the shader knows how much of the sun reaches it, shadow included. Full sun is the flat colour. Where the light turns away, a halftone screen of dots grows until it closes into the shade colour. A cast shadow is the shade colour under a fine dot screen of a deeper shade. The core of a form shadow (the side turned from the sun) is hatched toward ink. Lanterns and flashes add one hard cel band of their light on top. The patterns are in screen pixels, as a printed tone is on the page, but are pinned to a world point so a pan does not drag the world under fixed dots. Shadow maps are snapped to a hard band, so cast shadows are shapes.
- Flat fills. No PBR textures, no HDR environment, no roughness maps. Wood, plaster, paving and tile are painted colours from one palette (`PALETTE` in `src/world/compound.ts`); the only textures are flat flagstone and plank patterns drawn on a canvas at start.
- Brush ink. A mask pass reads the world's depth and the arms' depth and marks a fine line at every crease and a full one at every silhouette. Each mark carries the width the brush wants there: heavier on a silhouette, heavier the deeper the break behind it, heavier toward the camera, and swelling and tapering along the line with a pressure fixed to the world. The page gathers the marks over a small disc, so each line is as wide as its own pixel asks, and splits a light-pressure stretch into dry-brush bristle streaks along the stroke. Nothing carries its own outline mesh; one hand inks everything.
- Paper. The frame is printed on warm paper with a fibre grain, colours a touch quieter than the render, inside a hand-ruled panel border.
- Staging, as a panel would draw it: focus lines converging on a big hit, speed lines along a dash or a Shift, an impact frame drawn in two inks that flips positive and negative, and a sound word lettered over a heavy hit in the Technique's colour (`sfx` on the kit, `word` on a cutscene). Effect rings are ensō brush circles and smears are dry-brush strokes (`src/render/brush.ts`).
- Painted moments. A cutscene shot can switch the whole page (`page` on a `CutShot`): sumi-e ink wash (the frame as three wet ink tones along brush strokes, the Technique's colour the one accent), watercolour (colour bled along strokes, pooled at its edges, lifted toward the paper), or a negative (white ink on a black page). Recoil and Shift stay in line work: lines rush in on the wind-up, focus lines hold on Recoil's blow, and Shift's blow is all speed. Rime's glacier lands as cold watercolour. Blink's Eyes Shut is a negative on the blow. Echo's resonance and the Master's Five Thousand Years play as sumi-e from the wind-up on. The Enforcer's Flashpoint holds focus lines on the blow and bleeds into watercolour as the ground burns.
- Quality. The High preset gathers the brush over a 3-pixel reach (at 1080 lines) with dry brush, paper fibre and the painted looks; Medium a 2-pixel reach; Low a 1-pixel ink line with tones but no dry brush, fibre or painted looks.
- Stylized people. One rigged human (Quaternius, CC0) in flat skin, flat hair and a flat suit in the role's colour, with the head and hands drawn large. No skin textures and no face detail. Each enemy role wears its own costume over the suit (`src/character/costume.ts`): the thug a red headband and a wide belt, the bruiser spiked iron pauldrons and a topknot, the leaper a long scarf, the husk a pointed hood and a cape, the dummy a sack hood, a target and a crossbar, the Kuroda Enforcer an open jacket with fuel tanks, the Master a white robe with bell sleeves. A role reads by its silhouette from the top-down camera and by its clothes up close. `tools/check-tints.mjs` keeps the suits apart from skin tones and from each other, gives every role its own costume, and keeps every costume piece out of skin tones.
- Late afternoon. A low warm sun from the west, long hard shadows, blue shade, a painted sky gradient with flat cloud bands, lanterns already lit. Mood from light, not from detail count.

The motion and fight direction stay at the level of MAPPA's Jujutsu Kaisen fights:

- Strong anticipation, fast snaps, and overshoot. Every ability has a readable silhouette.
- Impact frames on big hits: one to three frames drawn in two inks, positive then negative, with focus lines on the point hit, then a hold.
- Hit-stop scaled to the blow. Smear and motion-trail VFX on fast limbs.
- Debris and shock rings that follow real physics.
- Short cinematic camera moves for Maximum Output: a dolly-in, a dutch angle, a brief slow-mo, and a cut back to gameplay control, all skippable.

Hand gestures are choreographed like key animation, not tweened between two poses. References to judge against: the JJK S2 Shibuya fights (Gojo vs Jogo, Yuji vs Mahito) for motion; for the look, a colour page of a weekly shōnen manga (screentone shade, brush ink, focus lines and lettered sound words), Okami's sumi-e, and Hi-Fi Rush and Guilty Gear Strive's cel characters.

## Stack

- Vite + TypeScript (strict). three.js 0.170.
- Physics: `@dimforge/rapier3d-compat` (WASM inlined, so it works on GitHub Pages). Use the kinematic character controller for the player and NPCs, and dynamic rigid bodies for props.
- Rendering: `postprocessing` (pmndrs) for bloom, a hue-preserving tone curve, SMAA and the page (the ink mask pass, the arms' pass and the page effect). Toon materials painted in screentone; no ambient occlusion pass and no HDR environment.
- UI: Preact + @preact/signals for menus and HUD. The DOM sits over the canvas. Never build HTML strings.
- Assets: only CC0 or similarly permissive assets, committed under `public/assets/`, each listed in `CREDITS.md` with source URL and license. In use: Quaternius (the rigged human and its animations), Kenney (furniture, crates, barrels, sounds) and KayKit (crates). Geometry is meshopt-compressed. The first load is under 2 MB.

## Core data shapes

Each organizing structure is chosen so that adding content means adding data, not adding branches.

```ts
// App flow: one state machine, not booleans.
type AppState =
  | { kind: 'boot'; progress: number }
  | { kind: 'title' }
  | { kind: 'tutorial'; step: TutorialStepId }
  | { kind: 'explore' }
  | { kind: 'workshop' }            // Pact workshop UI open, world paused
  | { kind: 'encounter'; id: EncounterId }
  | { kind: 'paused'; resume: AppState };

type CameraMode = 'top' | 'third' | 'first';  // V cycles, with an eased transition; top is the default

// Techniques: a registry keyed by id. An ability is data plus an effect function.
interface TechniqueDef {
  id: TechniqueId; name: string; color: number; resource: ResourceDef;
  abilities: Record<AbilitySlot, AbilityDef>;   // 'primary' | 'secondary' | 'max'
}
interface AbilityDef {
  gesture: GestureId;                            // drives the hands and body
  phases: { windup: number; active: number; recover: number };
  cost: number;
  effect(ctx: AbilityContext): void;             // acts on the physics world and actors
}

// Hands: poses are data. A gesture is a timeline of poses blended with springs.
interface HandPose { wrist: Euler; fingers: Record<Finger, [number, number, number]>; armOffset: Vector3 }
interface Gesture { keys: { t: number; left: HandPose; right: HandPose }[]; camera?: CameraKick }

// Pacts: a composable rule, evaluated each hit. Programming is IF/THEN rules.
type Condition =
  | { kind: 'still' } | { kind: 'range'; max: number } | { kind: 'facing'; deg: number }
  | { kind: 'healthBelow'; frac: number } | { kind: 'named' } | { kind: 'grounded' } | { kind: 'airborne' };
interface Pact {
  id: string; name: string;
  sacrifice: SacrificeTag;          // anti-loophole: a tag already paid for earns nothing more
  condition: Condition; benefit: { output: number };
  duration: 'permanent' | { seconds: number } | { encounters: number };
  risk?: { extends: Condition; cost: Cost };
  wager?: { declare: Prediction; reward: number; forfeit: Cost };
}

// World: props are data with physical and Technique-response traits.
interface PropDef { model: string; mass: number; breakable?: { pieces: string; hp: number }; traits: PropTrait[] }
type PropTrait = 'freezable' | 'resonant' | 'flammable' | 'porous' | 'kinetic-source';

// Tutorial: a data-driven step list, each with an objective predicate.
interface TutorialStep { id: TutorialStepId; title: string; body: string; objective(g: GameView): boolean; highlight?: UiAnchor }
```

## Systems

1. **Player and camera.** A Rapier character controller handles walking, sprinting, jumping, mantling small ledges, and the dash. Three camera views, cycled with V and chosen in Settings (`src/player/camera.ts`); switching eases the camera from where it was to where the new view wants it.
   - **Top-down (default).** The classic arena's high angle: the camera hangs above and behind a point between the player and the cursor and always looks north, so up on the screen is north on the map. No pointer lock: the cursor is visible, the body faces it, and Techniques fire at the point under it on the ground. Movement is screen-relative (W is up the screen whichever way the body faces) and sprint works in any direction. Roofs and walls that stand between the camera and the player fade out, and whatever is dressed on a faded roof hides with it, so a fight in the hall is as readable as one in the yard.
   - **Over the shoulder.** Pointer-lock mouse look turns the body; the spring arm collides with the world; the player aims at the crosshair.
   - **First person.** Viewmodel arms in a separate overlay pass with their own FOV, plus head bob, sway, landing dip and FOV kick; aim at the crosshair. The arm rig sits ahead of the eye, as viewmodels do, so poses are in reach with the elbow bent and the forearms rise from the lower corners; a wrist past 93% of the arm is drawn back toward the shoulder, so a lunge never locks the arm straight down the line of sight behind its own sleeve. The overlay keeps its 16:9 horizontal field in a narrower window, and a cuff finishes each sleeve at the wrist.
   The same rigged character plays locomotion clips through an AnimationMixer, with a gesture layer on the arms and chest that plays the Technique's gesture (see **Third-person gestures** below). `PlayerController` keeps the facing (`yaw`) and the movement heading (`moveYaw`) apart; `tools/check-controls.mjs` proves the keys move along the camera's axes in every view. Input (`Input`) and player state (position, velocity, yaw, moveYaw) stay plain data a network layer can read and apply.
2. **Hands.** Use rigged, skinned first-person arms with finger bones, either from a CC0 source or built from a skinned mesh. Never use capsules. Each Technique has signature gestures, for example:
   - Recoil braces both palms forward to absorb, then throws a punch.
   - Rime's nails frost over and grow claws, and it slams a palm to the ground for Icebreak.
   - Blink cups its eyes, and the hands flinch back on the pulse.
   - Echo presses a palm flat to a surface, and Ring is a snap.
   - Shift makes a two-finger point before translating.

   Idle hands breathe and flex a finger now and then.
3. **Techniques in the world.** Rime freezes the koi pond into a floor you can stand on and pins props. Recoil absorbs falling or thrown props and relaunches them. Echo resonates a resonant prop or structure until it fractures into pre-broken pieces. Blink shoves props and people. Shift translates through walls and must handle occupied destinations.
4. **The compound.** One walled dojo compound, 48 by 40 m inside the walls, hand placed to the last post (`src/world/compound.ts`). A full lap takes well under a minute. It contains:
   - The training hall on the north side: the Pact Architect's desk (the Workshop), the dummies at the back, the Master's mat. Its flat roof deck is the rooftop trial's floor, reached by the stair on its west side.
   - The courtyard: the sparring ring (sand, a stone kerb, a painted boundary) where the sparring partners fight, the koi pond, the hoist for the Recoil crane drill, stone lanterns, benches, trees, crates and barrels for the Techniques to throw.
   - The storehouse on the east wall, its roof joined to the hall roof by a plank bridge.
   - A wall walk along the east and south walls from the storehouse roof to the gatehouse roof, and a stair down beside the gate. The Kuroda Enforcer answers a challenge at the gate.
   - Outside the walls: meadow, a tree line and painted hills, seen from the roofs.

   Every surface is complete: walls, roofs and the stair are closed solids, the roofs have rails and the walls an invisible rail above them, so nothing floats, nothing shows a seam and nobody falls out. Lore plaques with short wiki excerpts are collectible.
5. **Combat.** Use the same rules as the arena version (Pacts, Resolve, Risk, Wagers on R, Maximum Output on Q, Eyes, Aura Expansion and Aura Clash on F). Enemies are rigged and animated, with hit reactions and ragdoll-lite knockback through physics impulses.
6. **Pact Workshop.** Lesson from the arena version: its random offer of "Unmoving Stance (+50% while still)" next to "Rooted Vow (+60% while still)" was a non-choice. Anything that presents Pact options side by side must offer distinct sacrifices, never two that share a sacrifice tag. Every modal must fit the viewport at 1280×720 and 1920×1080 with nothing clipped; the capture harness checks this.
    Build a Pact from parts: sacrifice, condition, benefit, duration, and an optional Risk or Wager. Show Resolve live, including the anti-loophole rule ("this sacrifice is already counted"). Programming is a small IF/THEN rule builder. A training dummy room lets you test Pacts.
7. **Tutorial.** Run it in the courtyard as a step machine, covering movement, aiming, the camera views, the primary and secondary abilities, the extra slots, a Pact, a Wager, and Maximum Output. It can be skipped and replayed from the menu. `tools/check-flow.mjs` plays it from the first step to the last with real keys and the mouse.
8. **UI.** Treat the UI as a design system. It has a title screen with a slow orbit over the compound, a loading screen with real progress, a HUD that stays out of the way, and a settings menu covering sensitivity, FOV, quality preset, volume, the camera view, and invert Y. The pause menu holds a codex and a map of the compound. The UI is fully keyboard navigable, with transitions on every surface. Japanese-influenced typography, used tastefully.
9. **Audio (issue #14).** Layer CC0 recordings and Web Audio synthesis: an evening bed that changes indoors, footsteps per surface, a sound for every Technique's casts and Maximum Output, hit weight, the aura and clashes, drills, UI, a sparse score, and master, music and effects volumes.
10. **Performance.** Hold 60 fps on an M1-class laptop on the High preset, with a Low preset. Use instancing for repeated props, frustum culling, and texture budgets.

## Delivery

**Phase A, foundation (one owner).** Put every system in place, playable end to end, at the highest quality one pass can reach. Keep module boundaries clean along the gauntlet pieces below, so each piece can be improved without touching the others.

**Phase B, gauntlet loop.** The lead splits the game into independently improvable pieces:
- first-person hands and gestures
- third-person character animation
- the district's look
- Technique VFX and fight direction
- encounters and enemy animation
- UI
- tutorial and workshop
- audio

Each piece gets a builder agent and a separate critic agent. The critic judges recordings and screenshots against the named real-world references and writes concrete failures. The builder revises until the critic passes it. Nobody grades their own work.

Delete code once it is superseded. Do not keep it alive alongside. Commit each phase after it builds and passes the headless checks. Keep the `?debug` hook: expose `window.game` and add teleport and skip-tutorial helpers.

**The proof-of-concept pivot (#48).** The district, pedestrians, cars, neon, rain, fast travel and the realistic look were cut and their code and assets deleted. What remains is the power system and one finished area. Realtime multiplayer (#44) is built on top of this; see the Multiplayer section.

## Phase A deviations and known gaps

Each line names a departure from the sections above, with the reason.

- **One rigged human serves first and third person, drawn cel-shaded.** Every character is the Quaternius Universal Base Character (CC0) with clips from the Universal Animation Library (CC0), both retargeted and packed into `public/assets/characters/human.glb` by `tools/human.mjs`. The rig has three finger bones per finger. The first-person arms are the arm cut of that same mesh, driven by the pose and gesture data in `src/hands/`. The base character is unclothed, so the suit, sleeve and cuff are offset shells generated from the skin mesh at build time. At runtime (`src/character/avatar.ts`) every material becomes a toon fill (the file's skin textures are not used) and the head and hand bones are scaled up for drawn proportions; a look picks hair and skin tone, and an enemy's `tint` and `build` pick its suit colour and chest width. Costumes are rigid pieces (bands, open skirts, domes, boxes, cones) fixed to bones in the idle pose; the pieces on one bone are baked into one mesh with vertex colours, so a costume costs a few draw calls. The page's brush inks bodies, costumes and the first-person arms like the world; the arms render into their own target, which the page lays over the world and inks from its own depth.
- **Pacts are the issue #3 model, not the stat-buff table.** `src/pacts/schema.ts` is the single source of truth, with a validator and an exported JSON Schema for issue #16. Catalogue Abilities are pre-authored as example Pacts the player can inscribe or edit. Resolve shows as a number plus a qualitative read. Both open questions can change inside `src/pacts` and `src/workshop` only.
- **Technique kit contract.** Every Technique is one module exporting `TechniqueKit` (`src/techniques/kit.ts`). Two things still sit outside the kit: the Blink blindness overlay reads `blinkUi` directly in `src/ui/App.tsx`, and Rime's canal ice platforms touch the physics world from inside the kit. Both should move behind a kit `ui`/`world` hook when Tier 2 lands.
- **Mastery (issue #17)** lives in `src/mastery/mastery.ts` with its tuning in `MASTERY_CONFIG`. A drill hands out items at a rhythm (a crate off the hoist, a tell to answer), each scored once; two exist, the Recoil crane drill and the aura drill, Reading the Field, which any Technique can run. A stat can have a ceiling below 1 (`Mastery.capBy`): aura refinement stops at its potential.
- **The full hand sign (issue #42)** is a state on `Techniques`, `SignState`: idle, signing (with progress), primed (with time left). Alt (`fullsign`, rebindable) starts the kit's `sign` gesture. Nothing cancels it: the player is rooted for its length and cannot cast, Wager or open the Workshop, and hits land without interrupting it. A finished sign primes the Technique for `MASTERY_CONFIG.sign.window` seconds; the next cast plays the flick variant and its potency carries `Mastery.signBoost`, which rises with Activation from `boost.novice` to `boost.master` along `activation^curve`. Every kit's sign shares the 1.6 s shape in `signGesture`: anticipation, the seal at about 0.5 s, a hold, the release at 1.2 s, and a primed stance the hands hold until the cast or the window's end. In third person the camera plays a front three-quarter shot for the sign, and the body plays the same sign on its own arms (below). Maximum Output keeps its always-full sign in the cutscene. The old hold-Alt modifier and the `fullSignHold` setting are gone. `tools/check-sign.mjs` (`npm run check:sign`) drives it with real keys and saves the first- and third-person frames at the sign's beats; `tools/capture.mjs --only sign` shoots every Technique's sign for the hands critic.
- **Third-person gestures (issue #46)** drive every body's arms from the same gesture data as the first-person hands. `GestureClock` and the hand springs in `src/hands/performer.ts` are shared: `Hands` runs them for the camera, and `BodyGesture` (`src/character/gesture.ts`, through `Avatar.perform` and `Avatar.release`) runs them for a body, solves the wrists onto its arms with the same IK (`solveArm` in `src/hands/rig.ts`, calibrated on each body's own T-pose), and blends the result over whatever the locomotion clips play. Only the arms and the chest take it, so the legs keep their stride. Seen from outside, a first-person pose reads small, so a body plays a bolder cut: reach, spread and height from the shoulders are scaled up, the poses the camera cramps (a punch, a chambered fist, the seal at the sternum, a slam, claws, the hanging rest) are restaged in `STAGED`, and the chest turns into the hand that reaches further and leans with the reach, which gives anticipation and follow-through a silhouette. The hands are placed against the body's root, not its chest, so the clips' hunch does not sink a seal to the belt. The player's casts, the full sign, the short and flick variants, the Maximum Output sign in its cutscene, enemies' wind-ups and casts and the bosses' signs (`src/combat/moves.ts`), and the aura's expansion all play through it; online, a cast message carries its variant and speed, so every screen plays the same gesture, and a remote player's sign starts and lapses with its state. The clash stances (`Avatar.stance`) stay procedural on top. `npm run check:casts` (`tools/check-casts.mjs`) plays every Technique's moves from the top-down and over-the-shoulder cameras and fails one whose hands do not travel far on screen and stand off the torso, or whose impact pose matches another move's; it saves a strip of frames per move.
- **Cutscenes (issue #27)** are `CutsceneDef` data: each kit's in its kit file, the bosses' in `src/combat/ultimates.ts`. A def is a list of shots, each a camera move between two keys placed in the caster's facing frame relative to the caster, the target or the point between, a world time scale, and timed events (`gesture`, `title`, `resolveHit`, `impactFrame`, `hitStop`, `shake`, `vfx`, `sfx`); `opening` and `blow` build the beats every Output shares. One player, `Cutscenes`, runs them all: it pulls the target `pull` metres in front of the caster (short of walls, onto a floor), cuts between shots on its own timeline (a hit-stop holds the camera too), times the full sign so its strike meets the `resolveHit` beat, keeps every shot out of walls, and hands the camera back with `CameraRig.cutBack`, which eases to whichever view the player uses. Each cutscene runs 2 to 4 s. The player in one is rooted, takes no input, and is hurt only by its own blow (`hurtPlayer({ cutscene })`). The `cutscenes` setting plays them always, in full the first time and cut to 0.6 s after the blow afterwards, or never; Space skips the camera but the blow still waits for its beat, so skipping is never faster. A boss with an entry in `BOSS_ULTIMATES` reaches for it in place of a cast when `ready`, as the caster with the player as the target.
- **Aura (issue #4)** is its own section below.
- **Effect lifetimes (issue #53).** `Vfx` (`src/techniques/vfx.ts`) owns every effect: the pooled meshes, the later beats an ability schedules (`after`, on game time, never `setTimeout`), and anything an effect puts into the world for a while (`until`: the frozen pond's floor, a slowed stride). `Game.endScene` ends all of it, with frozen props, every kit's own state (a kit's `reset`), the enemies and the toasts, whenever a run starts, the rooftop trial begins or ends, or the Technique changes. `tools/check-effects.mjs` (`npm run check:effects`) proves a rooftop restart leaves nothing behind.
- **Workshop lesson.** The Workshop offers no side-by-side random options at all. Per issue #29 the readback sentence is the editor: each phrase the engine enforces is a button, the panel under the sentence shows that term's alternatives (a body diagram, direction tiles, range rings, a duration timeline, glyph tiles) with what each is worth to this player, and one bar reads Resolve. A sacrifice another Pact in use already pays shows struck through with that Pact's name. Every sheet is capped to the viewport and the capture harness checks the fit at 1280×720 and 1920×1080; `tools/check-flow.mjs` drives the whole flow with real clicks and times the 30-second test. `tools/check-keybinds.mjs` (`npm run check:keybinds`) drives the slots and rebinding with real input. Every check script takes the URL as a bare argument or `--url`, and serves `dist/` itself when it gets none (`tools/lib/target.mjs`).
- **Catalogue Abilities start inscribed (issue #3).** `PactBook` keeps an explicit binding per key. A key falls back to the raw Technique when the player unbinds it. Resolve is judged against the Pacts in use, so an unbound Pact's sacrifices stop counting. A draft can be tried on the dummies before it is inscribed.
- **Ability slots and key bindings (issue #41)** are two registries. `SLOT_DEFS` in `src/pacts/slots.ts` lists every slot: `primary`, `secondary`, `max`, the four extras `art1` to `art4`, and `passive` (no key). Each row names the kinds it accepts and its default key. A kind (`primary`, `secondary`, `max`) says how an Ability is cast, and `KIND_DEFS` holds its cooldown and base cost. A kit's `shapes` list the kinds they allow (`kinds`), and its gesture and audio tables are keyed by kind, so a Pact on `art2` casts as its shape's kind and no code branches on a slot. `ACTIONS` in `src/app/keymap.ts` lists every rebindable action, and each slot with a key is one. A binding is up to two cells per action (a main key and an alternate) holding a `KeyboardEvent.code` or `Mouse0` to `Mouse4`. No code sits in two cells: `assign` swaps or unbinds on a clash, and the Controls tab (`src/ui/Controls.tsx`) asks the player which. Bindings persist in `zenko.keys.v1`. Every label reads `keyLabel(action)` or `keyPhrase(action)`: the HUD, the Workshop readback and key picker, the tutorial cards, the key hints and the rooftop sheets. Escape is fixed, because it leaves pointer lock.
- **Auto-bind and save migration (issue #41).** A catalogue Ability binds to its own slot, or to the first free extra slot when another Ability holds it, so Shockline starts on 1. `PactBook` keeps a `claimed` list of catalogue Abilities that have had their turn, so one the player unbinds stays unbound after a reload. A save from before extra slots has no list: an Ability that holds its slot, or whose slot the player emptied, counts as placed, and the rest move to a free extra slot. Existing bindings are never changed. A Pact sits on one slot, so binding it empties the slot it left, and the Workshop offers swap or replace when the target is taken.
- **Rooftop offers (issue #41).** Offer templates name a kind. Each becomes a Pact on the slot that casts that kind, and, while an extra slot is empty, a second Pact on the first empty extra slot. The card reads "Adds to 2". Adding counts as stronger than any replacement, so fewer offers are pure sidegrades.
- **No PBR textures.** The look is flat fills and screentone; the only textures are the flagstone and plank patterns drawn on a canvas at start, and the Kenney palette maps the props arrive with. Geometry is meshopt-compressed. First load is under 2 MB. `tools/manifest.mjs` rewrites the byte manifest the loading bar reads whenever an asset is added or removed by hand.
- **Sound (issue #14)** lives in `src/audio/`. Gameplay asks for a named cue (`game.audio.play('recoil.punch.hit', { at })`) and never for a file; `cues.ts` is the whole table, so retuning a sound is an edit there. A cue is layers: Kenney recordings (one of several, picked at random) for the physical part, and voices from `synth.ts` (whoosh, boom, crack, riser, inhale, shimmer, tinkle, warp, tear, splash, taiko, a Karplus-Strong koto) for the energy around it. Techniques share one grammar: a cast cue under the wind-up, an impact cue on the frame it lands, and for a Maximum Output a charge under the sign, an inhale or rush on the cut before the blow, the blow, and a tail the kit plays when the effect fires. `Combat.hitEnemy` adds the body's side of a blow in four tiers by damage, with the weight scaling the boom. The mixer (`audio.ts`) is master, then music (the score), world (cues, the bed, two convolution rooms: the hall and the walled yard) and UI; the faders are `volume`, `music` and `effects` in settings, squared so they feel even. Nothing is created before the first click or key, and the recordings (one pack of mono Opus files, `audio` in the asset manifest, about 0.25 MB) are fetched only then and decoded one at a time, so the burst never competes with the game or the room. Cues placed with `at` go through a panner; the listener is the player's head, facing the camera's heading, in every view. Other bodies' footsteps come from their own velocity, and online a Technique's cues travel over the effects wire and play at the caster. The bed (`ambience.ts`) is wind with gusts, leaves, crickets, birds, the pond as a point source, a far temple bell; under a roof the outside is low-passed and the hall's room tone and boards come up. The score is a drone, a koto in the D In scale when calm, and taiko under a fight and faster in a clash; big cues duck the bed and the score, a cutscene holds them down, and Blink's blindness or low health muffles the world with a heartbeat under it. `src/audio/ui.ts` sounds the interface from what it shows: any button on hover and click, toasts, banners, plaques, the pause menu, the Workshop. `tools/check-audio.mjs` (`npm run check:audio`) reads the cue log (`game.audio()`, behind `?debug`) and renders cues offline (`game.audioRender`) to prove what cannot be heard headless.
- **Navigation (issues #33, #35), scaled to the compound** reads one registry, `POIS` in `src/nav/poi.ts` (name, kanji, where it is, label anchor, shrine). The compass, the tracked marker with its edge clamp, the ground trail (`src/nav/trail.ts`), landmark name labels, the map (a plan of the compound) and the shrines (`src/world/waypoints.ts`) all derive from it. The tracked target is the map pin, else the tutorial step, else the first goal in `GOALS` whose place has not been visited. A place counts as visited when the player stands within 5 m of its shrine; visits and the pin persist in localStorage. There is no fast travel: nothing in the compound is more than a short walk away. The compass reads the camera's heading, which is north in the top-down view. M opens the map; clicking a place selects it and Track pins it. The HUD margin is `--hud-pad`; the top-left column (`src/ui/nav/TopLeft.tsx`) holds the tracker and the key hints in one flow, and `tools/capture.mjs --only hud` checks the corner margins, overlaps and on-screen fit at 1280×720, 1920×1080, 2000×1000 and 2560×1440. `tools/check-navigation.mjs` (`npm run check:navigation`) drives the map, tracking, the marker and a visit with real input.
- **Rooftop trial (issue #34)** fights on the training hall's roof deck. It lives in `src/rooftop/`, one file per concern: `waves.ts` (the eight waves, scaling and score as data), `offers.ts` (the Pact templates and the rule that no two offers share a sacrifice, plus `audit` that rolls them), `arena.ts` (the trial's dressing on the deck: the painted ring, the marks where a wave climbs on, lanterns, cover and the board), `rooftop.ts` (the run as a state machine: fight, offer, over) and `RooftopUi.tsx` (run strip, Pact and Eye sheets, end screen). The title's Rooftop button and the board at the top of the hall stair both start it; the stair itself is walked. The offers are templates authored in the #3 schema that pair two or three sacrifices the engine enforces (trigger, range, direction, target, resource, standing still), plus any catalogue Ability the key is not using. Taking one inscribes and binds it for the run only: the book is exported at the start and imported at the end, and again at boot if a run was closed mid-way, so the trial never rewrites the player's real Pacts. An Eye taken after wave 3 is kept after the run (see Eyes below); the Eye sheet offers all four that can be installed alone and lets a player who carries one keep it. Regular enemies carry half their yard health and 65% of their damage, and the crowd takes turns to wind up (`Encounters.claimAttack`), because a wave is a dozen bodies where a yard fight is two. `tools/check-rooftop.mjs` plays all eight waves to victory in the top-down and first-person views and to defeat in the over-the-shoulder and top-down views, and asserts every offer; `tools/perf-rooftop.mjs` measures a full wave in all three views.
- **Eyes** (`src/eyes/`) are a separate axis from Techniques: finite artifacts, each installed in place of the right eye, for good, so the installed Eye is kept in `zenko.eye.v1` and goes with the player into the yard, the roof and online rooms. `registry.ts` holds the data; each Eye's way of seeing is a `Sight` (`horizon.ts`, `thread.ts`, `empty.ts`, `vault.ts`) that `Eyes` (`eyes.ts`, owned by `Combat`) runs every frame and asks on every blow the player lands. What a sight draws is on the bearer's screen alone: it uses its own marks (`draw.ts`), never the effect pool that online mirrors to the room. **Horizon** draws a body's next blow before its wind-up starts: the blow's own mark in gold where the body would stand to throw it, a ghost of the body there (its skinned meshes drawn again through the same skeleton) and the path to it; only the three soonest resolve and a crowd makes them fainter; a blow it saw coming that misses leaves its thrower's guard broken for 1.5 s. Before a boss cast it rules where the Kuroda's fuel would land or how far the Master's wave would reach. **Thread** ties each lighter body to the heaviest body within 7 m that clearly outweighs it (a boss reaches 9 m): a blow to that anchor runs 40% down every thread, what hangs on it takes 80% while it stands, and it reels when the anchor falls; the threads of the group under the aim are drawn heavier, and the Kuroda's pour hangs from him until it burns. **Empty** draws each guard as an arc on the floor with the gap left out behind it, gone entirely while the body recovers from its own blow, is thrown, frozen or held; a blow through the gap lands 1.5× and staggers; on the roof it also shows the hatch the next body will climb from. **Vault** holds a Technique other than the player's (rolled on the Eye sheet) and casts that kit's raw primary on G with a 2.4 s cooldown, its own half-full reserve and loose aim; the kit runs as if it were in hand for the length of the call, then the player's own comes back. Installing plays a short scene over the frame (`EyeInstall.tsx`: the lids close, one eye opens again in the dark in the Eye's colour, the lids part with that side washed), and any key ends it. The player's body wears the mark (`mark.ts`: an iris of the Eye's colour in the right socket and a short glint trailing back past the ear, which reads as a tick on that side from above); online it rides the player's state as `e` and every screen draws it on their body. In first person the right edge of the frame is ruled off and printed in two inks with the Eye's colour for paper (the page effect's `eyeK`). `tools/check-eyes.mjs` (`npm run check:eyes`) proves each Eye's rule in play.

## Multiplayer (#44)

Realtime rooms of up to eight players fighting in the compound, live, each with their own Technique and Pacts. Play Online on the title, or a link with `?room=<code>`, opens the Technique screen with a name field (kept in `zenko.name`) and the room code (default `dojo`). The address bar carries the room code once inside, and the online menu copies the link.

**Shape.** `server/realtime.mjs` hangs off the game server's port at `/ws` and keeps rooms in memory. The client side is `src/net/`: `protocol.ts` (the message shapes, the reference for both sides), `rules.json` (every number both sides share: tick rate, interpolation delay, health, respawn, bounds, spawn points; the server imports it too), `net.ts` (the socket, reconnect, and the bridge to the game), `remote.ts` (another player's body), `fxwire.ts` (effect mirroring), `propsync.ts` (the shared props). `src/ui/Online.tsx` holds the join form, name plates, scoreboard, feed and the downed card.

**Authority, and why.** Responsiveness and agreement under 50 to 150 ms matter more than cheating for now, so:

- Each client simulates its own movement, Technique, Pacts and Resolve, exactly as offline. Nothing about moving or casting waits on the network. The Pact engine runs unchanged on the caster's client, so it stays one deterministic module.
- The attacker's client decides whether its Ability hit, against other players drawn where it sees them (favour the shooter: what you aimed at is what you hit). Kits never learn they are online: another player is a `RemotePlayer`, which is an `Enemy` with no AI, so cones, spheres, rays against the enemy group, Wagers and the Maximum Output target all treat players like any other body. Its `hurt` sends a hit to the server instead of taking health.
- The server is the only place health changes. It checks each hit against bounds (both standing, the victim past spawn protection, within 48 m, a sane amount, a send-rate limit) rather than replaying the attacker's simulation. It then asks the victim's client what its own defences let through (Recoil's brace, Echo's quiet, the grace after a hit), because those are rules of the victim's Technique state, applies the answer once, and announces it to the room. A victim that does not answer in 1.5 s takes the full amount. Every hit carries a per-attacker sequence number and a server hit id, so a resent or duplicate hit never applies twice and an answer after the timeout is ignored. Since the attacker decides and the server announces, every screen agrees on who was hit.
- The server owns downs (health 0), the score (downs dealt, times downed), respawns (4 s later, at the spawn point farthest from everyone standing) and 1.5 s of spawn protection.
- Health a player spends on itself (Risk costs, a health-priced Ability) is sent as self damage and applied the same way.

**Movement.** Clients send their state 20 times a second, stamped with their own clock. The server relays everything received at a fixed 20 Hz tick. Receivers interpolate each player on that player's own timeline, 100 ms behind the newest sample (the offset is the least-delayed arrival over the last two seconds, so jitter and the tick's bunching are absorbed). A gap of more than 5 m between samples (a respawn, a Shift) is a cut, not a slide. Past the newest sample a body coasts on its velocity for at most 150 ms.

**Seeing Abilities.** Casts are announced with the caster's clock, so the other screens play the avatar's cast clip when the drawn body reaches that moment. Everything a Technique draws through the shared effect pool (`Vfx`: rings, sparks, trails, frost, ice, afterimages) is recorded and replayed on the other screens from the same calls, also on the caster's timeline, and so are the Technique's sound cues, placed at the caster. The pool is wrapped from outside, so kits are untouched. The attacker sees its number at once and the name plate's bar drops on the blow, settled when the server answers; a hit the victim's defences stopped reads "blocked".

**The shared world (#58).** Physics props are one simulation for the room, owner-authoritative per prop. A moving prop belongs to the screen that last acted on it: that screen simulates it and streams its pose 15 times a second, and every other screen holds it kinematic and eases it toward the stream, a little ahead along its velocity. A prop at rest belongs to nobody and sits at the same pose on every screen, the one its last owner set it down at. Every push a kit gives a prop goes through `PropSystem.push`, `launch` or `freeze`, which touch the prop first, so acting on someone else's moving prop takes it over and the blow lands where it was dealt; a prop moved by the player's own body, or knocked by another prop, is taken over the same way. The server decides ownership (the last claim it hears wins and everyone hears the change in its order), relays only the owner's poses, and keeps rest poses for whoever joins later. A player who leaves, or whose tab goes quiet for 1.5 s, hands their props to the lowest-numbered player still sending, who carries on from the last streamed pose, so a crate thrown just before a tab hid still comes down as one. Breakage is the server's: blows on a breakable prop are added up there, and the break is announced once, so the debris plays on every screen and nobody's count drifts. Rime's pond floor is an event: freezing it sends the seconds, and every screen lays the same one slab (several freezes hold one slab until the last ends). The new messages (`props`, `prop-hit`, `ice`) are bounded in `server/realtime.mjs` like the rest: at most 64 poses a message and 512 props tracked per room, every number clamped, prop blows on their own 40-a-second bucket, and one ice event per player each half second.

**Single-player assumptions, answered online (the list in #44).**

- *Pause freezes the world.* Online the menu, the map and the Workshop are overlays: the world keeps running and you can be hit (the menu says so).
- *Maximum Output freezes time.* Online it locks only its two people, like a clash. The caster sends `max` with the cutscene and the player it pulled in; the server checks the target is in reach and free, locks both until `max-end` or `rules.max.maxMs` (4 s), and refuses a second Output from the same player inside `rules.max.cooldownMs`. While locked, the server drops every hit and effect on either of them except the caster's own blows (at most `hitsPerTarget` on each body), and drops the target's. The target's screen plays the same cutscene from the receiving end and moves its own body into the shot; everyone else's world runs at full speed, they see the two glow and stand still, and the feed names the Output.
- *Hit-stop and slow-mo bend the clock.* `GameClock.shared` keeps time at its real rate online; the shake, kick and impact frame remain as local presentation.
- *Wagers, encounters, the aura clash.* A Wager counts hits on players. The sparring partners, the gate, the Master, the drills and the rooftop trial are single-player and are not offered online (the Workshop's dummies still work, locally). Aura Expansion works online as offline, and two players' fields that touch clash; see Aura below.
- *Saves.* The Pact book, mastery and bindings stay in this browser's storage and travel with the player into rooms. There are no accounts.
- *Client authority.* As above: checked against bounds, not replayed.

**Rooms and connections.** A room is created on first join and dropped once empty. A dropped socket keeps its seat and score for 20 s: a reload or a reconnect takes them back (one token per tab in sessionStorage). Choosing to leave gives the seat up at once. The server pings sockets to find dead tabs. A background tab stops sending, so others see it marked away and standing still, and its props pass to someone else; hits on it still resolve, because the socket keeps handling messages, and a tab Chrome has frozen outright takes the full amount after the 1.5 s timeout. If the server restarts, clients keep playing locally and rejoin with backoff.

**Known gaps.** Enemies and their encounters stay single-player, so nothing online shares their state. A prop let go with nobody else playing falls on each screen on its own until the next touch. A hit travels four network legs before health settles (attacker, server, victim, server), so with 120 ms each way a down lands about half a second after the blow on the attacker's screen; the name plate's predicted bar hides most of that.

**Checks.** `npm run check:multiplayer` serves `dist/` through `server/index.mjs` and drives two browsers in one room (three for the clash) through the join form with real keys and clicks: each sees the other walk and stop within centimetres of the true position with no frame-to-frame jump, one Ability damages the other exactly once by the amount sent, a down, the score and a respawn replicate, an Aura Clash between them plays the same steps and winner on both screens and on a third player's, who keeps walking through it, a player who leaves mid-clash loses it, a shoved crate is followed and comes to rest at the same pose on both screens and changes hands when the other shoves it, a crate both players hit breaks once on each screen, one player's Cold Stitch makes a pond floor on the other's screen, a tab sent to the background (reading hidden, no frames, timers throttled) is marked away, hands over the crate it was throwing, takes a hit while stopped and comes back in agreement, and a tab Chrome freezes still takes its hit, both rejoin after a server restart, and a player who leaves disappears. `--latency 120` adds 120 ms to every message in each direction (also a `server/index.mjs` option). `tools/perf-multiplayer.mjs` measures frame times in each view with seven protocol bots (`tools/lib/bots.mjs`) running round the player.

Rules for new work stay as before: no new global time changes, and game rules stay in deterministic modules apart from rendering and input.

## Aura (#4)

Aura Expansion and the Aura Clash, built to the owner's decisions on issue #4. Aura is its own faculty: it never fuels a Technique, and it works everywhere, offline and online, from the first wave of the rooftop trial.

**Shape.** `src/combat/field.ts` is the rules of one field (the pool, the open field, how it ended) and every number in `AURA`. `src/net/clash.js` is the clash referee, plain JavaScript so the server imports the same file the browser runs; `clash.d.ts` types it. `src/combat/aura.ts` owns the player's field, F, the fields of the Master and of other players, and what a field does to the bodies in it. `src/combat/clash.ts` plays a clash on this screen. `src/combat/fieldView.ts` draws a field and the clash seam. `src/drills/fieldReading.ts` is the aura drill. `src/ui/Aura.tsx` is the meter and the clash sheet.

**Refinement and potential.** Refinement is a mastery stat (per Technique, like the others) and sets the field: at 0 it reaches 4.5 m with density 0.3, holds 9 s and refills in 14 s; at 1 it reaches 7.5 m with density 0.9, holds 14 s and refills in 9 s. Its ceiling, the potential, is `1 - 0.1 × (breadth - 1) - 0.05 × Pacts inscribed for the Technique`, never below 0.2. Breadth is each kit's innate possibility space, from the wiki's "broader Technique, lower aura aptitude": Blink 1 (a forward pulse), Recoil and Rime 2, Shift 3, Echo 4 (vibration through anything connected, air included). With no Pacts, Blink can reach 100 and Echo 70, and every Pact inscribed costs 5. Trained refinement above the potential is kept but held to it, so removing a Pact gives it back. The HUD's aura meter and the mastery row both read refinement against potential. The drill, Reading the Field, is at the listening stone in the yard in front of the hall: the stone's field shows a clash tell, the player answers with the pick that beats it, and three skill-gated tiers (5, 7 and 9 tells; 1.4, 1.0 and 0.75 s to answer) train refinement toward the potential. It doubles as the clash tutorial.

**Expansion.** F expands and spends the whole pool. F again retracts at any time, and the share of the field's time left unused stays in the pool, so an early retract brings the next expansion sooner. A field that runs out or collapses leaves the pool empty. Either way, F does nothing until the pool is full. The three ends read differently: a retract draws the field in with a soft ring and a toast; a field that runs out fades; a collapse cracks white, throws shards, shakes the camera and shows 乱れ, and the meter turns red.

**What a field does.** The numbers are small, because a field is open often. Inside your own field you sense and act faster: wind-ups play up to 13% faster, and your hits on bodies in the field land up to 18% harder (both scale with density). A body inside someone else's field, without a field of its own open, moves `1 - 0.5 × density` as fast, and an enemy winds up that much slower: 15% at the weakest field, 45% at the strongest, 30% inside the Master's. A field does not stop Techniques and gives no guaranteed hit (the wiki: dominant territory is pressure and sensing, not negation).

**The look.** The boundary is a painted band on the ground in the field's colour, an ink line just inside it and a dashed edge outside it, with pressure rings drifting in toward the owner, faster the denser the field. That ring is what reads from the top-down camera. A dome over it has a hard rim band from outside; from inside, its far wall is a low painted curtain of contour lines, so it never washes the frame. Indoors the dome stops at the ceiling, and a dome wall fades out in the last few metres before the camera. In a clash the side-on shot stands at the edge of both fields, so the domes keep only a trace and the paint inside each ring thins; the ring edges carry the boundaries and the tells (`check:aura` measures the wash in the hall). The Master's far wall still draws only for a player inside her field (`check:effects`). Other players' fields are drawn from the radius and density they send with their state.

**The clash.** Two open fields whose edges touch start a clash. It locks only the two in it: they stand, cannot cast and cannot be hurt, and their camera cuts to a side-on two-shot with letterbox bars. The clock is never touched, and anyone else keeps playing at full speed. There are up to three exchanges, first to two. In each one both sides pick, hidden: push (W) beats hold (S), hold beats feint (A or D), feint beats push. The same pick goes to the denser field when the gap is at least 0.1, and otherwise to nobody; no pick within 1.2 s counts as hold. Level after three, the denser field wins if the gap is at least 0.02, and otherwise both fields break. A clash takes about 4 s: 0.7 s to meet, then each exchange closes as soon as both have picked. The Master shows her pick 0.3 s into an exchange (0.45 s into the third), with her whole body because her robe hides her legs (`Avatar.stance`, shaped to read in profile from the clash shot): before a push she leans in with both palms driven out level and her field swells toward you, before a hold she settles back with her forearms crossed before her chest and her field draws tight, and before a feint she darts half in and out with her lead hand high and her rear arm swept back while her far side flickers; the sheet says it in words too. Players take the same stances when they pick. `check:aura` reads her hands in her own frame for each tell. She favours pushing, pushes more after losing an exchange, and never makes the same pick three times running. Her density is 0.6, so an untrained player loses tied picks to her and a refined one wins them.

**After a clash.** Nobody's health changes. The loser's field collapses: their pool refills from empty, they are held for 0.9 s once the cutscene ends (the Master for 1.35 s), and for 3 s every blow on them lands 20% harder. That is the winner's opening; the winner's field stays open. When both fields break, nobody is held.

**Online.** Each client sends its field's radius and density with its state. A client whose open field touches another player's asks the server (`clash`, at most once a second per asker). The server starts a clash only if both players stand in the room, neither is in a clash, and the two reported fields touch at the reported positions, with 1.5 m of slack. It then runs the referee on its own clock and sends every step (`clash-step`) to the whole room, so both players and everyone watching play the same picks and winner. Picks (`clash-pick`) are checked against the clash, the round and the three picks, and the server's window is 250 ms longer than the one on screen. A player who disconnects or goes down mid-clash forfeits it. A clash sends no health messages: each loser's own client collapses its own field, because the rules of a player's state live on that player's client.

**Checks.** `npm run check:aura` plays it offline with real keys: F expands and spends the whole pool; F retracts with no refund; F does nothing at half a pool; a field runs out; bodies in the field are slowed and hit harder; the drill raises refinement from the cue on screen; potential falls with breadth and with one more Pact, and holds refinement under it; and two clashes against the Master, one lost and one won, with no health lost, the clock at full speed, and a thug outside the clash still walking. `check:multiplayer` covers the clash between players. `tools/perf-rooftop.mjs` measures a wave with the field open and a clash in the hall.
